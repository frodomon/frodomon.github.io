industry: "Retail · Community Commerce · Entertainment"
client: "Self-initiated venture — Founder"

context: "El Tablero de Otto began as a personal observation, not a brief. As an active member of the Magic: The Gathering community between 2012 and 2014, I kept hearing the same complaints from players living across traditional Lima neighborhoods (Pueblo Libre, San Miguel, Magdalena, Jesús María, San Borja, Miraflores, Surco, San Isidro): no store near home, available formats demanded high investment, the experience at the two dominant stores of the time was poor, and there was no comfortable space to play. At the same time, the generation that had discovered TCGs in the early 2000s was now economically independent and eager to play again. With a strategic partner who had a location facing PUCP, I decided to build the store that community was asking for — not as just another retail business, but as a service design exercise where, beyond designing the experience, I was the business."

problem: "The business problem was never selling cards — any store can do that. It was giving someone a reason strong enough to leave home and stay three or four hours somewhere else, week after week. Through personal interviews, I found that 'Magic players' weren't one homogeneous audience but four distinct archetypes, behaving very differently along two variables: risk aversion and disposable capital for the hobby. Semi-professional players wanted to qualify for international events and rarely bought sealed product. Amateur players played for fun, with no interest in competing. New players wanted to learn without a large upfront investment. And pseudo-competitive players only showed up when there was a prize on the line — I deliberately chose not to design for this last group: their behavior undermined the play environment that sustained the other three segments. Deciding who not to serve was the single most important decision of the whole project."

role: "As founder, I was simultaneously business designer, service designer, brand strategist and community manager — and responsible for the full operation: supplier relationships, product import, accounting, legal business registration, web development and customer service protocols. I ran the full discovery process (contextual observation, mystery shopping at competitor stores, in-depth interviews), defined the four archetypes and mapped their distinct needs and purchasing behavior, designed the brand identity and event formats for each segment, and designed and iterated the community infrastructure — archetype-specific WhatsApp groups, a Facebook community for card trading, live streaming of tournament finals. I ran the business as a continuous design experiment: launching trial formats, measuring engagement, and iterating on observed behavior rather than assumptions."

approach: "The core strategic insight was that the store itself wasn't the product — the community was. Before designing a single event, I mapped what players wanted across two layers: the explicit layer (parking, comfortable numbered tables, good ventilation, food and drinks without leaving the venue, good ambience) and the implicit layer (belonging to a healthy community, kind treatment, finding players at their level, feeling the space was special, making friends). Designing only for the explicit layer builds a store; designing for both builds a community that doesn't leave for a better price elsewhere. From there I designed a four-phase customer lifecycle engine: Segment Acquisition (each archetype with its own channel — competitive events with international qualification for semi-professionals, free workshops via Facebook Ads for new players, casual formats for amateurs), Living the Otto Experience (delivering both value layers on the sales floor), Otto Lover (bringing returning players into the brand's community groups), and Otto Ambassador (incentives for 'Lovers' to bring new players, restarting the cycle through organic acquisition instead of paid). Community infrastructure — archetype-specific WhatsApp groups, Facebook for trading, tournament livestreams — kept the cycle alive between events. The business model rested on a simple finding: competitive players spent little on product but drove traffic and visibility; amateurs spent more and sustained revenue. When we expanded into Pokémon TCG, the same archetype framework held and revealed a new profile — the collector — validating that it was a reusable system, not a one-off solution. During the pandemic, the same segmented-community logic let the hobby move to digital platforms without losing the group identity that sustained it."

impact:
  - "El Tablero de Otto became one of Lima's leading TCG stores — validated by community demand, not marketing spend"
  - "Sustained organic attendance growth driven entirely by peer referral, across all four archetype segments"
  - "Four parallel event tracks (Sealed, Draft, Pauper, FNM), each with distinct format, pricing and prize structure"
  - "Network expansion through partnerships with other Lima TCG stores that joined the mission to grow the player community"
  - "The archetype framework, originally mapped for Magic, was successfully reused when expanding into Pokémon TCG — validating it as a system, not a one-off solution"
  - "Established the frameworks later applied at institutional scale: archetype modeling, service blueprint design, community-as-product strategy and iterative event design"
